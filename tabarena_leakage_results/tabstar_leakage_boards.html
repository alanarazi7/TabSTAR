TabSTAR Leakage Boards

How TabArena turns per-dataset errors into an Elo, what the other leaderboard columns add, the same 87-method board re-scored on classification, binary, multiclass and regression tasks with each method's position inside each board, and a dataset-by-dataset view of where the leak shows.

Companion to the leakage report. Same run, same result files, nothing re-fit.

The three arms

Three fine-tunes of the same TabSTAR default configuration, differing only in the pretrained checkpoint. TabSTAR's pretraining corpus has 400 datasets, 32 of which are TabArena datasets. Five leave-one-fold-out checkpoints were each pretrained on 320 of the 400, excluding a different fifth.

TabSTAR-Default-NoBug-WithLeakage-400The released checkpoint, pretrained on all 400. On the 32 in-corpus datasets it saw the test dataset during pretraining.
TabSTAR-Default-NoBug-NoLeakageOn each in-corpus dataset, the one fold checkpoint that excluded it. On the 19 off-corpus datasets any checkpoint is leakage-free, and fold k0 was used.
TabSTAR-Default-NoBug-WithLeakage-320On each in-corpus dataset, a fold checkpoint that still included it: the same corpus size as NoLeakage, but leaky like the 400. It has no off-corpus counterpart, so on those 19 datasets its row is filled from TabSTAR-Default-NoBug-WithLeakage-400.

The 320 and 400 leaky arms are near-equivalents; the leak is what separates either of them from NoLeakage. TabArena's default would fill the missing rows with RF (default), which is a much weaker model and would drag the 320 arm down; this page fills from another TabSTAR arm instead. Filled cells are marked.

Elo versus the full leaderboard

TabArena's leaderboard is sorted by Elo, but Elo is only one of its columns and it answers a narrow question: who beats whom, how often. It never looks at the size of a win. The other columns fill that gap. On this page "#" always means a method's position in the board sorted by Elo, while "mean rank" is its average position on individual tasks.

How the Elo is computed

Every task (here one dataset, split r0f0) stages a round-robin. Each pair of the 87 methods forms one "battle" and the lower metric error wins, so one task yields 3,741 battles and the 51 tasks together yield 190,791. A Bradley-Terry model is then fit to all battles at once by logistic regression: each method gets a strength such that the probability of A beating B is 1 / (1 + 10(B − A) / 400). That is the chess Elo formula, so a 400-point gap means a 10 to 1 expected win ratio and a 100-point gap roughly 64 to 36. The strengths are shifted so that RF (default) sits at exactly 1000; it is board position 77 of 87 on the all-tasks board, so most methods land above 1000. Each task carries total weight one, however many folds it has. The ± interval is a 95% bootstrap over tasks: TabArena re-draws the 51 datasets with replacement 200 times and refits, and the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the refitted Elo become the bars.

Two consequences matter for reading the arms. First, Elo is invariant to margins: beating a method by 0.0001 AUC counts exactly as much as beating it by 0.1. Second, the fit is joint, so adding or removing methods moves everyone's Elo a little even though the ordering between two fixed methods almost never flips.

What the other columns add

#
Position in the board sorted by Elo. This page shows it per board, so a method has one position on the all-tasks board and another on each task-type board.
Elo, +, −
Bradley-Terry rating as above, with the upper and lower half-widths of the 95% bootstrap interval.
mean rank
On each task, rank all 87 methods by error (1 = best, ties share the average), then average across tasks. Sensitive to how many methods sit between two rivals, not just who wins.
win rate
Share of all battles won, ties counting one half. Close to a monotone transform of Elo but not identical, because Elo also credits whom you beat.
improvability
Per task, 1 − best error / own error, averaged. The share by which a method's error would have to shrink to match the best method on each task. Zero means it was best everywhere. This is the margin-aware column: two methods can have the same Elo and very different improvability.
filled
Share of tasks where the method has no result of its own. Only TabSTAR-Default-NoBug-WithLeakage-320 has such tasks: the 19 off-corpus datasets, 37% of the all-tasks board, filled here from TabSTAR-Default-NoBug-WithLeakage-400. TabArena's leaderboard files call this column "imputed" and fill from RF (default).
time train / infer
Mean seconds per task for the fit and for the prediction. For the arms this is the 8-fold bag on one A100 spot node.

Worked example: WithLeakage-400 against NoLeakage

On the all-tasks board TabSTAR-Default-NoBug-WithLeakage-400 sits at Elo 1073 (position 67) and TabSTAR-Default-NoBug-NoLeakage at 1043 (position 72). The 30-point gap corresponds to an expected 54 of 100 battles between the two, well short of the direct head-to-head count of 21 to 11 on the 32 datasets where both have a leak-relevant comparison. The joint fit reads each arm's strength from its battles against all 86 opponents, not from the two arms' own duel, so a rivalry that is lopsided in isolation can still be nearly even on the Elo scale. The 400 arm's bootstrap bars (+67 / −70) swallow the gap, because the boards move by more than 30 points when a few datasets are re-drawn. The mean ranks are 60.5 versus 63.0, so on a typical task the two arms sit about 2.5 rank places apart. The win rates are 30.9% versus 28.0%. Improvability is 26.1% versus 27.8%, so once margins count, NoLeakage is 1.6 percentage points further from the per-task best than the leaky 400: a small and consistent effect, not a rank collapse.

Dataset by dataset: where the leak shows

Elo needs many tasks. On a single dataset with one split, the comparable quantity is each arm's rank among the 87 methods on that split, so the table below gives, for every dataset, the rank of the three arms and of TabArena's own TabSTAR (default) entry, how many places each leaky arm gained over TabSTAR-Default-NoBug-NoLeakage, and by what share it cut NoLeakage's error. For AUC tasks the error is 1 − AUC, for the rest RMSE or log loss.

What counts as a large gain? The 19 off-corpus datasets say. There the 400 checkpoint and the k0 fold checkpoint are both leak-free and split their duels 10 to 9, yet on a single dataset they differ by up to 40 places and 15% of error. On seismic-bumps the k0 checkpoint is rank 6 and the 400 rank 46 for a 5% error difference, which is what a dense stretch of the board does to a rank; on hazelnut-spread-contaminant-detection a 15% error difference moves only 5 places. Rank and error therefore have to be read together. A gain exceeding all 19 control gaps would arise by chance about once in 20 draws, so a dataset is flagged only when both leaky arms beat NoLeakage by more than every control gap.

By rank that flags QSAR_fish_toxicity (NoLeakage rank 62, the 400 and 320 arms rank 3 and 1, gains of 59 and 61 places). By error it flags diabetes (the 400 and 320 arms cut NoLeakage's error by 23% and 32%, ranks 43 against 5 and 4) and kddcup09_appetency (the 400 and 320 arms cut NoLeakage's error by 38% and 36%, ranks 85 against 65 and 67). Next in line, above the control's 90th percentile of 19 places or 7% of error but within its range, are diabetes (38 and 39 places) and qsar-biodeg (23 and 32 places) by rank and anneal (19% and 12%) and credit-g (17% and 10%) by error. Across the 32 in-corpus datasets both leaky arms beat NoLeakage on 19, both lose on 8 (most clearly on blood-transfusion-service-center, splice and website_phishing) and 5 are split, so the leak is a broad small shift with a few large outliers rather than a uniform boost. The 320 arm is the best of all 87 methods on 2 in-corpus datasets (QSAR_fish_toxicity and qsar-biodeg); on diabetes the best method is TabArena's official TabSTAR (default) entry, which loads the same released checkpoint as the 400 arm and leaks the same way.

Source. Per-split results written by TabArena's compare step for this run (results_per_split.csv, 87 methods × 51 datasets, fold 0 of repeat 0). The per-type boards were produced by feeding subsets of those rows to TabArena's own scorer, bencheval.BenchmarkEvaluator, with the settings its leaderboard reporter uses (Elo calibrated to RF (default) = 1000, 200 bootstrap rounds). Fed the full set with TabArena's default fill, it reproduces the run's tabarena_leaderboard.csv Elo, mean rank and win rate exactly; the boards here differ from that file only in filling the 19 missing TabSTAR-Default-NoBug-WithLeakage-320 rows from TabSTAR-Default-NoBug-WithLeakage-400 instead of RF (default). In the data files the arms keep TabArena's raw keys TabSTAR_base, TabSTAR_correct and TabSTAR_wrong for the 400, NoLeakage and 320 arms. The per-dataset table ranks the same rows directly (per_dataset_leakage.py), with dataset sizes from TabArena's curated dataset metadata. Scripts and outputs sit next to the run's other evaluation files in the git-excluded eval/tabarena_leakage_analysis folder.

Caveats. One split per dataset, so the bootstrap bars are wide and positions within a few points of Elo are interchangeable. Official TabSTAR entries were run by TabArena on their own hardware and protocol; the two protocols differ (this run lets TabSTAR make its own validation split instead of receiving AutoGluon's, and the official entry was produced on other hardware with its own time limits), which is the most likely reason the 400 arm outranks the official default entry on identical splits.
